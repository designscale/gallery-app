Section 01 — Problem Statement

The gap no one has filled

New York City has one of the most vibrant and accessible gallery ecosystems in the world — and almost no good way to navigate it spontaneously.

Over 200 galleries in Chelsea alone — all free to enter, most open Tuesday through Saturday, with a concentrated pulse of openings every Thursday evening. Yet despite this richness, discovering what's on, where it is, and how to get there remains genuinely hard.

The experience today: someone wants to spend a Thursday evening in Chelsea. They open Google, search "gallery openings Chelsea tonight," and land on a mix of five-year-old listicles, paywalled editorial, and newsletter archives. Artsy shows them what's for sale. Artforum requires fluency in contemporary art criticism just to parse the listings. Time Out buries galleries under restaurants and Broadway shows. Instagram is real-time but fragmented across dozens of individual gallery accounts.

"What's free, what's open, and where do I walk tonight?" — No single tool answers this question.

Section 01 — Opportunity

The right interface for the right moment

The gap is not a lack of information — it's a lack of the right interface for the right moment. Gallery-hopping is an inherently mobile, in-motion, spontaneous activity. The person who needs this app is standing at the corner of West 22nd Street and 10th Avenue at 6:30 PM on a Thursday, phone in hand, wondering which direction to walk first.

The opportunity is to build a product that serves that exact moment: fast, location-aware, beautifully minimal, and oriented entirely around free discovery — not commerce, not criticism, not collecting.

The Insight

The barrier to going is not distance, cost, or availability. Every gallery is free. Most are a 10-minute walk from each other. Openings happen every Thursday. The barrier is not knowing that you're welcome — and not knowing how to find out.

Section 03 — Product

Key features

Seven features, each one designed around removing a specific friction that stops the primary user from going.

01
Tonight View — The Default Answer

The app opens to the current date and immediately shows what's opening tonight and what's on view this week. No setup, no login, no location permission required. Opening receptions are clearly marked with time, gallery, artist, and show — everything needed to decide in under 60 seconds.

02
Chelsea Map — Know Before You Go

A full-screen interactive map centered on the West 20s gallery district, with clear visual distinction between galleries hosting openings tonight versus ongoing shows. Designed for spatial decision-making: see what's clustered, understand the walking distance, plan a two-hour route without consulting a second app.

03
Gallery Detail — Full Picture, One Tap

Every gallery card opens a detail sheet with the show title, artist, opening time, weekly hours, a short gallery description, and a direct link to Apple Maps or Google Maps directions. The information hierarchy is designed for someone standing on a sidewalk, not sitting at a desk.

04
Explore & Search — Find What You're Looking For

Browse by neighborhood or search by gallery name, artist, or show title. Useful for following up on a recommendation ("I heard there's a Muholi show somewhere in Chelsea") or discovering what a specific gallery is showing before committing to the walk.

05
Before You Go — The Insider Briefing

The feature no competing platform offers and that the primary user needs most. A short, warm, non-condescending pre-visit guide — specific to gallery openings, not generic etiquette advice. It covers everything she's been quietly wondering about.

Dress code
Stylishly casual. Think nice dinner with a creative friend. All-black is always safe. Comfortable shoes — you'll be on concrete for two hours.
Arrival
Walk in. No invitation, no reservation, no sign-in. The door is open. That's the whole point.
The prosecco
Yes, there's usually wine. Yes, it's free. Hold it, sip it, don't overthink it.
Looking at work
Slowly. Stand in front of things. It's not a test. You don't need to have an opinion out loud.
Talking to people
Artists and gallery staff are often in the room and happy to talk. "What can you tell me about this piece?" always works.
How long to stay
Fifteen minutes is fine. An hour is lovely. No wrong answers.
When to leave
Whenever you're ready. A quiet exit is completely normal.
Everything else tells her where to go. This tells her she belongs there.
06
RSVP & Event Registration — Confirm Your Spot

Most Chelsea gallery openings are walk-in, but an increasing number — artist talks, collector previews, special programming — require a reservation. Openings surfaces RSVP status immediately on every event card: a clear "Walk Right In" or "RSVP Required" tag, with registration one tap away.

Rather than a proprietary system, Openings routes directly to where the event lives:

🟣 Luma
🟠 Partiful
🌐 Gallery Direct

She's a professional — she RSVPs to things. Knowing whether she needs to, and doing it in one tap, signals that the app respects her time.

07
Free, Always — No Friction, No Paywall

Every gallery in the app is free to enter. That fact is surfaced immediately and consistently on every card, because uncertainty about whether entry costs money is one of the small frictions that stops the primary user from going at all.

Section 04 — Landscape

Competitive analysis

The current market offers several partial solutions, each with a distinct blind spot against our target use case.

Artsy

The largest online art marketplace, originally built around gallery and auction discovery in New York. It has evolved toward a transactional model — buying, selling, and collecting — and its UX reflects this. Discovery is organized around works for sale, with gallery listings as secondary context. For a user who wants to know what's opening Thursday night and how far a walk it is, Artsy requires navigating past a lot of purchase intent to get there.

Key gap: Commerce-first architecture buries event and opening discovery.
Artforum / Artguide

The publication of record for the contemporary art world. Its Artguide tool provides the most comprehensive international exhibition listings available. However, it is designed for readers — people with enough art-world context to navigate the listings fluently. The mobile experience is not optimized for in-motion use, and the interface assumes cultural fluency that excludes a significant audience of curious but less expert visitors.

Key gap: Editorial depth without mobile-first usability. High barrier to entry.
Time Out New York

Covers gallery openings as part of its broader arts and entertainment vertical, alongside restaurants, nightlife, theater, and film. The coverage is accessible in tone and well-curated, but galleries compete for attention with every other category. The experience is generalist by design — a user looking specifically for gallery discovery must filter past significant noise.

Key gap: Gallery discovery as one of many categories, not the organizing principle.
NYC-ARTS / GalleriesNow / Manhattan Monday

Newsletter-first products — weekly email digests curated by editors who know the New York gallery circuit deeply. The curation quality is high and the tone is right. The fundamental limitation is format: a static weekly email cannot serve a spontaneous Thursday evening decision. There is no map, no real-time context, no way to filter by what's open right now.

Key gap: Right content, wrong format. No interactive or real-time layer.
See Saw Gallery Guide

The closest existing product to the concept described here — a dedicated gallery discovery app for iOS. See Saw offers gallery listings, hours, and show information with a map view. However, it has not been updated to reflect the current gallery landscape, has limited coverage outside major markets, and lacks the event-specific layer (openings, receptions, talks) that distinguishes a planned visit from a spontaneous one.

Key gap: Closest competitor but stale data and no event-first design.
